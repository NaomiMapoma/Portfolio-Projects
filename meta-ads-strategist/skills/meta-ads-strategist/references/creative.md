# Creative

- **Last verified:** 18-11-2026
- **Confidence:** Medium
- **Used by:** SKILL.md workflow step 4; `diagnostics.md` sections on fatigue, CTR and delivery concentration

## Purpose and scope

Covers creative diversity, formats, fatigue signals and testing frameworks.
Read when the user asks about creative volume, concepts, testing, refresh timing or creative performance.

## Key principles (stable)

1. **Creative is a primary lever for who sees your ads and how well they perform.** With broader targeting and more automation, the ad itself does more of the work. See `audiences.md` and `delivery-and-algorithm.md`.
2. **Diversify concepts, not just versions.** Meta's own guidance, as reported, is outcome-based: diversify your creative, test, and let the system optimise. Small edits to the same ad are weaker than genuinely different ideas or concepts.
3. **Volume without difference doesn't help.** Many near-identical ads can behave like one.
4. **Every creative decays.** Fatigue is gradual and expected, so plan the pipeline before performance drops.
5. **Test to learn, not just to find winners.** The aim is to understand which concepts, hooks and messages work, and why, so you can make better creative next time.
6. **Judge creative on the business outcome.** Leading indicators (hook rate, CTR) help diagnose, but the deciding metric should be measured on meaningful business outcomes.
7. **Don't read single days.** One bad day is likely noise. Look for sustained patterns.
8. **Creative supply limits scale.** More budget needs more distinct concepts. See `structure-and-budget.md`.
9. **Compliance is part of the brief.** In regulated sectors, claims and disclosures must be built in before production, not fixed afterwards. See `policy-and-compliance.md`.
10. **Platform tools and metrics change.** Treat names and features (Advantage+ creative, diversity indicators) as dated facts. If unsure, check Meta official documentation

## Creative diversity

**What Meta has said**
- Meta's reported guidance is that creative diversification helps advertisers get the best results. A Meta spokesperson described its guidance as deliberately outcome-based and not model-specific: diversify your creative, test, and let the system optimise.
- Meta-reported figures circulate (e.g. that Advantage+ creative improved ROAS, or that one ad set with many creatives outperformed several ad sets). Treat them as aggregate claims, not guarantees, and find the original source before quoting.

**Concept vs variation**
- **A new concept** changes the core idea: the problem addressed, the angle, the persona, the format, the visual environment or the story.
- **A variation** keeps the same idea and changes surface details: a headline, a colour, a text overlay, a cropped version.
- Five different opening lines on the same shot, background and pacing is usually one concept.

**Dimensions to vary**

| Dimension | Examples | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Message / benefit | Save money, save time, reduce risk, status, peace of mind | Check claims for compliance in regulated sectors |
| Audience / persona / angle | Different life or buying stages (product-aware, problem-aware, solution-aware), needs or use cases | Persona differences should show in the ad, not only in targeting |
| Format and content | Static, video, carousel, UGC / creator, partnership / creator ads | See Formats |
| Visual environment | Studio, real-life setting, screen recording, animation | Visual sameness is the quickest way to blur concepts |
| Hook | Question, bold claim, problem, social proof, curiosity | Hook changes alone may not be enough to count as a new concept |
| Offer / proof | Discount, trial, testimonial, reviews, data, expert | |
| Tone | Humorous, serious, emotional, practical, educational | |
| Length and structure | Short, long, story-led, list, before and after | |

**Buyer journey stages matter**
- Your audience is not one group, they are at different stages of their buying journey and each stage needs a fundamentally different message.

| Stage | Audience Mindset | Ad Objective | Best Formats
|---|---|---|---|
| Early Journey | Problem aware, not solution aware. They feel the pain but haven't started looking or shopping | Validate the problem. Create curiosity. Make them realise solutions exist. | Anti-ad, Meme, News areticle, Reddit post, IG story, Video |
| Mid Journey | Solution aware, not brand aware. They know solutions exist but haven't heard of your product / service or brand. | Introduce your brand / product / service. Differentiate and build credibility. | UGC, Comparison, Us vs Them, Founder story, Listicle |
| Late Journey | Brand aware, not yet converted. They know you exist but haven't pulled the trigger. | Overcome objections. Create urgency and close the sale. | Data / Infographic, Text message, Before / After, Reviews |

**How much is enough?**
- The right volume depends on budget, conversion volume and production capacity. A smaller number of truly distinct concepts beats a large number of lookalikes.

**Diversity in regulated sectors**
- Compliance can narrow what you can say. Diversify through format, persona, setting and storytelling while keeping claims and disclosures consistent and approved.

## Formats

| Format | Strengths | Weaknesses | Use when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Static image** | Fast to produce, easy to test, clear single message | Less able to hold attention or tell a story | Clear offers, simple messages, concept testing |
| **Video** | Stronger hook and storytelling; suits vertical placements | Higher production effort; hook rate matters | Explaining a product, showing outcomes, demo |
| **Carousel** | Multiple products, steps or benefits in one unit | Needs strong first card | Storytelling, step-by-step, feature lists |
| **Collection** | A hero image or video with product tiles that opens a full-screen, fast-loading Instant Experience for browsing. Built for mobile product discovery. | Mobile placements only. Needs an Instant Experience and a product set | You want shoppers to browse a range within the app, such as fashion, home or beauty, and you can supply strong hero media. Not suitable if desktop delivery matters. |
| **Product media (Advantage+ catalog ads)** | Generates ads automatically from your product catalog, so it scales across large inventories without manual design. Can show different products, formats and media to different people. Suits retargeting and always-on sales. | Creative quality depends on the catalog (images, titles, prices, availability). Less control over what's shown and how it's cropped. Limited room for brand storytelling or a distinct concept. Mostly relevant to product catalogs (retail, travel, auto, property), less for services or lead gen. | You have a clean product feed, a large or changing range, and a sales objective. Pair with concept-led ads rather than relying on the catalog alone. |
| **UGC / creator-style** | Authentic feel, social proof | Quality varies; compliance and rights checks | Trust-building, testimonials, product use |
| **Partnership / creator ads** | Borrows creator credibility | Needs creator coordination and approvals | Awareness and trust |
| **Meta's AI-generated variations (Advantage+ creative)** | Fast variations of existing assets | May produce edits you don't want; check brand and compliance | Adding variation at low effort |
 
**Format guidance**
- Design for the placements you're using. Vertical formats (e.g. 9:16) are commonly used for Stories and Reels, and 4:5 for feeds. Check Meta's current specifications and safe zones.
- Put the key message early. Many people see the first seconds only.
- Design for sound-off with captions where appropriate.
- Collection is a **layout** and Advantage+ catalog ads are an **ad type**. They aren't separate either/or options. Within Advantage+ catalog ads, the available formats include collection and carousel (and single image), and Advantage+ creative for catalog can show different formats to different people.

**Automated enhancements**
- Check what automated edits are switched on (text variations, image enhancements, generated backgrounds, music). In regulated sectors, automated changes to claims or imagery can create compliance risk. See `policy-and-compliance.md`.

## Fatigue signals

**What it is**
- Performance decline caused by the same people seeing the same creative too often, or the creative simply losing appeal over time. It's gradual and can be caught early.

**Signals to watch**

| Signal | What to look for | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Frequency** | Rising week on week while reach stalls | Not a problem on its own. It matters when paired with falling performance. |
| **CTR** | Falling over consecutive days | Commonly treated as the main early warning |
| **Hook rate** (video) | Share of impressions reaching 3 seconds, declining | Often drops before CTR |
| **CPM** | Rising on stable or growing impressions | Can also reflect auction or seasonality |
| **Cost per result** | Drifting up | Lagging signal; by then spend is already wasted |
| **Conversion rate** | Falling | If it falls without creative decay, check the landing page and tracking |
| **Budget share** | A previously strong ad loses spend share without edits | Delivery shifting away can be a signal. Verify before concluding. |
 
**Rules of thumb**
- The ads that you should be pausing are the ones that have a high CPA, high frequency (1.8+) and low engagement ratio
- A single low day is usually noise. A steady decline over 7 to 10 days is more meaningful.
- Compare recent periods (e.g. 7 days) with a longer baseline (e.g. 30 days).
- Ads Manager may show a "creative fatigue" flag, but it tends to lag behind early signals.

**Distinguish from other causes**

| Looks like fatigue but may be | How to tell |
|---|---|
| Audience saturation | High frequency across all creatives and a small reached audience |
| Seasonality or auction pressure | CPM rises across the account; same-period history |
| Landing page or tracking issue | CTR fine but conversion rate falls; check events |
| Learning reset or structure change | Recent edits; see `delivery-and-algorithm.md` |
| Normal budget-share shift | New ads taking spend; check whether results hold at campaign level |
 
**Responses**
- Add genuinely new concepts, not just edits of the tired ad.
- Rotate in tested concepts from your pipeline.
- Rebalance structure or audience if saturation is the issue. See `audiences.md` and `structure-and-budget.md`.
- Keep a baseline of proven ads running while you test.
- See `diagnostics.md` for the symptom flow.

## Testing frameworks

**Purpose**
- Build a repeatable system that produces learning and a steady pipeline of tested concepts.

**Principles**
1. **Test concepts first.** Biggest differences in performance usually come from the idea and angle, not small tweaks. Then test hooks, formats and offers within winning concepts.
2. **Write a hypothesis for each test.** For example: "Message X will lower cost per qualified lead among audience Y because Z."
3. **Isolate what you're testing.** One main variable at a time where possible.
4. **Define the success metric and the decision rule in advance.** Prefer cost per meaningful outcome. Use hook rate, hold rate and CTR to diagnose, not to decide.
5. **Set minimum spend or event volume per ad** before judging.
6. **Give each concept a fair test.** Delivery tends to concentrate on some ads, so tests inside a normal campaign may not give every ad a fair chance. Use a dedicated testing campaign structure or Meta's A/B test tool where suitable. See `structure-and-budget.md` and `tracking-and-measurement.md`.
7. **Don't edit mid-test** and don't call winners early.
8. **Document every result,** including losses and inconclusive tests.

**A simple testing cycle**
```
1. Research and hypotheses   → customer insight, past results, competitor and category review
2. Brief and produce         → distinct concepts, compliant by design
3. Launch in a test structure→ fixed budget per concept, defined success metric
4. Read results              → after planned spend/volume; check uncertainty
5. Decide                    → graduate winners to scaling campaign, iterate promising, retire losers
6. Record learnings          → feed the next brief
```
 
**Graduation rules**
- A concept graduates to the scaling campaign when: spent 3-5x target CPA at or slightly above target CPA and high engagement ratio
- A concept is retired when: they have a high CPA, high frequency (1.8+) and low engagement ratio
- A promising but inconclusive concept: If frequency for an ad is low (1.3), that shows the ad is top of funnel being served to new audiences, introducing the brand. If, on that top of funnel ad, there is good engagement despite a higher CPA, then the audience is finding a lot of value in that ad it should probably be kept live.

**Organise the learning**
- Tag each ad by concept, persona, format, hook and offer so you can analyse patterns, not just ad-level results.
- Use consistent naming conventions. See `structure-and-budget.md`.
- Keep a learning log: what was tested, result, confidence, next step.

**Reading results**
- Report the difference and the uncertainty, not only "B won".
- A winning test doesn't guarantee the same result at scale or with a different audience.
- Look at downstream quality as well as volume, especially for lead gen.
- Check whether a win came from more favourable delivery rather than a better ad.

**Advanced**
- Use lift tests to check whether creative changes affect incremental outcomes where the decision justifies it. See `tracking-and-measurement.md`.
- For regulated sectors, plan approval time into the testing calendar so the pipeline doesn't stall.


## Decision guidance

- **If** the user asks "how many creatives should I run?" **then** ask about budget, conversion volume, production capacity and sector. Emphasise distinct concepts over a number, and say any specific figure is practitioner opinion.
- **If** a user has many ads that share the same template, background or structure **then** suggest they probably count as one concept, and recommend consolidating and briefing genuinely different ideas.
- **If** one ad takes most of the spend **then** don't conclude the others are bad. Check whether they were meaningfully different and whether they had a fair test. See `delivery-and-algorithm.md`.
- **If** CTR is falling and frequency is rising **then** suspect creative fatigue, but check for audience saturation, seasonality and tracking before acting. See `diagnostics.md`.
- **If** CTR is fine but conversions are poor **then** look at the landing page, offer and tracking before changing creative.
- **If** hook rate is low **then** fix the opening seconds before investigating anything further down the funnel.
- **If** a test result is based on little spend or a short window **then** say it's inconclusive and recommend continuing or redesigning.
- **If** the user wants to scale budget **then** check there's enough distinct creative to support it.
- **If** the user is in a regulated sector **then** plan compliance review into the brief and calendar, and avoid automated edits that could change claims. See `policy-and-compliance.md`.
- **If** the user relies on a specific "similarity score" claim **then** ask where it comes from and label it as industry interpretation unless it's from Meta.
- **If** the user asks about AI-generated creative **then** advise checking brand, accuracy and compliance of generated variations, and keeping humans responsible for approval.
- **If** creative testing needs to be measured causally **then** point to A/B or lift testing. See `tracking-and-measurement.md`.

## Common mistakes

- **Mistaking variations for new concepts:** same template, different text.
- **Producing volume without diversity.** 
- **Judging creative on CTR or hook rate alone** instead of the business outcome.
- **Calling winners early** or reacting to single days.
- **Testing too many variables at once.**
- **Waiting for ROAS to drop before refreshing.**
- **Treating high frequency as a problem on its own,** without checking performance.
- **Using fixed thresholds as rules** (e.g. "pause at frequency 3") without checking the context.
- **Scaling spend without scaling creative supply.**
- **Not recording results,** so learnings are lost.
- **Letting automated edits change claims or imagery** in regulated sectors.
- **Designing for one placement** and letting automatic cropping handle the rest.
- **Ignoring compliance until the end,** causing rejected ads and delays.
- **Pausing all "underperforming ads":** this can lead to the breakdown effect
